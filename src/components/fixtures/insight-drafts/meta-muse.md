## What it is, in plain language
A chatbot answers. An **agent** acts: you tell it what needs to get done, and it works out the steps and does them, coming back to you when it needs a decision.

Meta launched **Muse** on **September 8, 2026**. Meta calls it a personal AI agent, and it's designed so anyone can use it with no technical experience. In plain terms:
- **You talk to it like you'd text a person,** in the Muse app or directly in WhatsApp. It isn't strictly turn-by-turn, so you can send follow-ups without waiting or repeating yourself.
- **It has its own secure computer in the cloud,** called Muse Secure VM, with its own browser. That lets it open websites, fill out forms and keep working after you close the app.
- **It connects to your apps,** such as Gmail and Google Calendar, through what Meta calls Connectors. You choose what it can see and do.
- **It asks first.** By default, Muse checks with you before important actions like sending an email or making a purchase. It also shows you an audit trail of what it has done and plans to do.
- **It doesn't see your passwords or card numbers.** Credentials go into secure storage. For purchases, Muse can check out with Link by Stripe, which generates a one-time card so your real card details stay hidden.
- **It makes things.** Muse can read, summarize, edit or create PDFs, spreadsheets, documents, images and even podcasts, and saves them to a Library tab.

Muse runs on Meta's Muse Spark model.

**A note for business owners:** Meta positions Muse as a *personal* agent for everyday life. As of Nov 17, 2026, we couldn't find any team or business plans for it, and its examples lean personal (trip planning, shopping, bills). It's still a very low-cost way to learn what delegating to an agent feels like, especially for owner-run businesses where work and personal admin overlap. Keep sensitive customer data out of it until you've read Meta's Muse terms and privacy policy and are comfortable with them.

## How to get started
1. **Check availability.** As of Nov 17, 2026, Muse is available in the **United States** (since Sep 8) and **Australia** (since Nov 17). You can use it on iOS, Android and the web at [muse.ai](https://muse.ai). Meta's Australia announcement also lists a Mac app.
2. **Sign in with a Meta account.** If you don't have one, you can create one with a Facebook or Instagram account, an email address or a phone number. You must be 18 or older to subscribe.
3. **Start chatting** on the Chat tab. Use side chats (menu icon, then the pencil icon) to keep topics separate, for example one for each client or project. Your Muse stays aware across all of them.
4. **Connect your apps.** Go to **Settings > Connectors**, tap Connect, review the information and authorize. Or just say "Connect my Gmail." Many connectors can be set to read-only, so Muse can look but not act. If you already have Facebook, Instagram or Threads in the same Accounts Center, those connect automatically.
5. **Set your approvals** in Settings. Decide when Muse must ask you before acting. Keep sending and purchasing on "ask me" to start.
6. **Optional: chat in WhatsApp** by starting a chat with your Muse agent there (supported countries, latest WhatsApp version). You can also use voice in the app where it's available.

## Recommended setup: a "chief of staff" for your calendar and inbox
**Step 1: Connect Gmail and Google Calendar.** For email, choose whether Muse can only read your mail or also send on your behalf. Start with read-only. You can change this in Settings any time.

**Step 2: Tell Muse about your business** in one message: what you sell, your hours, your key customers and suppliers, and how you like to write. Muse remembers what matters, and you can tell it to forget specific things later.

**Step 3: Give it the job.** Copy and adapt this:

> Every weekday at 7:30 AM, send me a short plan for the day: my calendar, anything that conflicts or is double-booked, and the 5 most important emails I haven't answered (customers and payments first). For each email, draft a reply in my voice and wait for my approval. Never send, buy or delete anything without asking me. If a meeting has no agenda, draft one. Only look at my work email, not my other accounts.

That last sentence follows Meta's own tip: define boundaries clearly, for example "only search my email, not my text messages."

**Step 4: Add reminders.** Tell it, for example, "Remind me every Friday at 3 PM to send invoices." You'll see these under the Assistant icon, in Upcoming.

**Step 5: Review and correct.** Ask for changes in plain words ("shorter," "more formal with new clients"). Meta also suggests asking Muse to add a note that a message was generated by your AI agent when it writes to others, so recipients know.

**Step 6: Build a Space.** Spaces are custom experiences you create inside Muse by describing them. Try a "weekly business dashboard" or "event planner" Space to keep one project's work in one place.

## Pricing tiers (as of Nov 17, 2026)
Meta measures usage in "Muse tokens." Think of them as fuel for the agent's work. Your allowance resets **weekly**. Subscriptions renew monthly, and you can subscribe in the mobile app or at muse.ai. Meta notes that benefits may vary by region and account, and that subscriptions are still in limited testing.

| Plan | Price | Weekly usage | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | **$0** | Limited free usage that refreshes over time (amount not published) | **Everyone starting out.** Learn the basics with one or two tasks |
| Power | $20/mo | 500 million Muse tokens per week | Owners who use Muse every day for inbox, calendar and research |
| Maximum | $100/mo | 3 billion Muse tokens per week | Heavy users running long, multi-step projects such as research, negotiations and big planning jobs |

Meta does not publish how many tasks a token allowance covers, because it depends on the task. When you hit the free limit, you can upgrade, wait for it to refresh, or (according to WhatsApp's help page) make a one-time purchase of extra usage through Muse.

## Ideas to spark your own
- **Trades and home services:** Muse watches your calendar, spots back-to-back jobs on opposite sides of town and suggests a better order.
- **Retail:** Muse compares prices from suppliers or online stores and fills out reorder forms, then waits for your OK before buying.
- **Restaurants:** have Muse plan a tasting-night menu and draft the invites, or ask it to watch a booking page and reserve a table for a team or supplier dinner when one opens up (an example WhatsApp gives). The OpenTable connector can help where it's available.
- **Creative businesses:** use the Canva connector (available worldwide) to produce social graphics or a launch flyer from a short description.
- **Service providers (coaches, trainers, therapists' front desks):** Muse drafts appointment reminders and rescheduling replies for you to approve.
- **Agencies and freelancers:** Muse turns a client brief into a project plan and a spreadsheet timeline in your Library.
- **Owners lowering costs:** Meta says Muse can work on lowering a bill. Point it at a software or utility bill and ask it to research cheaper options and draft the request.
- **Selling equipment:** Muse can help sell something for more. Use it to list a used van or old equipment and negotiate, with your approval at every step.
- **Content:** turn a saved Instagram post into a plan, such as a recipe reel into a shopping list or a competitor's post into content ideas.

## Your setup to grow into
- **Free:** Connect Gmail and Calendar read-only and run the daily plan for two weeks. Note the time you save.
- **Power ($20/mo):** Upgrade when you hit the free limit regularly. Add more connectors (Canva and others, where available), turn on sending approvals for routine replies, and give Muse a standing weekly project.
- **Maximum ($100/mo):** For owners who hand Muse big, ongoing goals, such as sourcing a new supplier, planning an event end to end or running a long negotiation.
- **Know when to look elsewhere:** if you need staff seats, shared team workspaces or business admin controls, we couldn't find those in Muse as of Nov 17, 2026. That's the point to compare business-focused agents.

One caution worth repeating: when Muse launched, Reuters reported that Meta's internal tests had found the agent sometimes stalled and exposed sensitive data without authorization. Meta has since built in the approval and isolation safeguards described above. Keep approvals on, grant the least access a task needs, and check its work.
